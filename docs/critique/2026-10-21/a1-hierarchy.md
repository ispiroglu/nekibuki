# A1 — Visual hierarchy, composition & recognition

Target: Nekibuki, impeccable/integration @ a9952f7, http://localhost:5190
Method: isolated design review (Assessment A1), inspected 390x844 light + dark (login, onboarding ×3, camera rest state, camera settings shelf, report top / trivia / annotation sheet / chat, Places list, Profile), plus 320x568 and 1280x800 spot-checks of report / camera / profile. Playwright + Chrome fake camera, mock auth, demo data. No detector run.
Layer score: 2/4
Nielsen: H6 Recognition rather than recall = 2/4, H8 Aesthetic and minimalist design = 3/4
Audit dimension: Implementation Integrity = 3/4

Design specificity: Partly authored, partly category-interchangeable. The report is genuinely written for this product and could not be lifted into another app — a full-bleed photo carries numbered markers that map one-to-one onto a "What you are looking at" list, the intro is a photo-led lede, four trivia cards sit under "Things most people miss", and every place keeps its own conversation. The onboarding art argues the same thesis in miniature (viewfinder bracket → photo with facts → a question that stays attached to a place). But the two surfaces a traveller meets most often are generic: the camera is stock shutter/gallery/flip chrome with no Nekibuki-specific affordance at all, and Places is a thumbnail list with a search field. The mark itself is also expressed two ways — solid ink ring on the login, a 15%-opacity ghost ring inside the onboarding viewfinder — so the identity weakens exactly where the app is trying to establish it. Verdict: authored for this product at the report, category-interchangeable at the camera and the history list.

Strengths:
- The report reading column is a genuinely composed editorial surface: full-bleed photo → brand "kind" chip → serif H1 → one meta row → a lede in Newsreader at 1.375rem/1.55, then quick-fact tiles, trivia, and numbered references. The squint test yields photo → title → section in the right order, and the photo is allowed to be the only colourful thing on screen.
- The login is a real brand moment, not a form: wordmark, mark, the tagline promoted to a display headline ("A quiet guide for the places you walk past."), and exactly one action. Dark mode keeps the same composition and the petrol accent does its job.
- The onboarding set has a clear art → headline → body → step → action hierarchy, "Skip" is reachable without reading, and the copy earns its place ("no typing, no searching").

Findings:

### [P1] The viewfinder settings shelf has no visible trigger — long-press only, signalled once for 5.2 seconds
- Location: `/` (camera panel) — `src/screens/camera-screen.tsx:88` (`longPressMs = 420`), `:463-469` (long-press opens the shelf), `:932-955` (the only real control is `sr-only` and only becomes visible on `:focus`), `:334-340` (the hint is shown once per session and killed after 5200 ms), `:1003` ("Hold for settings" copy).
- Evidence: `shots/a1/50-camera-rest-nohint.png` — after the hint expires, the frame carries seven visible chrome islands (avatar, Location chip, clock, gallery, shutter, flip, nav pill) and **nothing** that opens torch / grid / zoom. `shots/a1/51-camera-shelf-longpress.png` — a 900 ms press in the dead centre of the frame is the only way in; the shelf it opens contains a single "Grid" pill. `shots/evidence/camera--mobile--light.png` shows the same screen while the hint is still up: the instruction exists only in the first five seconds of a session.
- Impact: torch, grid and zoom are invisible to every touch user after ~5 seconds, and are not discoverable at all on a second visit (the hint is a per-session `useRef`, not persisted). A traveller in bright sun who needs the grid or the torch has no path to them; the failure is silent rather than erroring, so it reads as "the app has no such feature".
- Fix: put a persistent, visible control in the HUD — a small sliders glyph in the existing top bar next to "Location on" (44×44, `glass-dark`, same `hudEdge` treatment) that opens the same shelf; keep the long-press as a shortcut and keep the one-time hint. Never ship a feature whose only entry point is `sr-only`.
- Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

### [P1] Tapping a photo marker opens a modal that repeats the article verbatim — the photo→article path dead-ends
- Location: `/report/demo-eiffel` — `src/components/report/photo-hero.tsx:206-249` (annotation sheet, eyebrow "IN THE FRAME", body = `active.body`) vs `src/screens/report-screen.tsx:326-338` ("What you are looking at" list, body = `reference.body`).
- Evidence: `shots/a1/71-report-annotation-sheet.png` — tapping marker 1 opens a dark glass sheet reading "IN THE FRAME / The open lattice / 18,038 iron pieces joined by 2.5 million rivets. The gaps are not decoration…". `shots/a1/11-report-trivia.png` — scrolling 300 px lower, list item 1 under "What you are looking at" reads the identical sentence. The sheet shows no crop, no zoom, no region of the photograph; the only photo-specific element is the mint dot that is already active behind it.
- Impact: the one interaction the product is built around — pointing at a detail in a photo — produces exactly the same information as scrolling, only behind a focus trap. A traveller who taps a dot expecting to be shown *what they are looking at* gets a duplicate of a paragraph and has to close the sheet and scroll to find it. It also inflates cognitive load (H8): the same fact now exists in two places with two different visual treatments.
- Fix: make the sheet earn its place — show the cropped region of the photo around the marker (the report already carries `reference.x` / `reference.y`), cropped and zoomed inside the sheet above the text, and shorten the body to one line with a "Read in the guide →" link that scrolls to the matching numbered item. If a crop is not feasible, drop the sheet and make the marker a scroll-to-anchor instead of a modal.
- Suggested command: /impeccable distill

### [P2] The camera landing screen is compositionally empty — eight islands around a frame with no anchor or instruction
- Location: `/` (camera panel) — `src/screens/camera-screen.tsx:826-930` (top bar), `:1011-1066` (shutter row); the preview itself is an unframed `<video>` with no guides.
- Evidence: `shots/a1/50-camera-rest-nohint.png` and `shots/a1/05-camera-landing-light.png` — with the fake camera the frame is a flat green field; the only mark inside it is a 44 px focus reticle sitting at dead centre (barely visible against the video). There is no framing rectangle, no rule-of-thirds grid, no "point at a monument" cue, no scan line. The screen's visual weight is entirely in the chrome: three controls top, three bottom, one nav pill.
- Impact: after onboarding the app opens on a screen with no composition and no verb. The user's eye lands on the shutter because it is the largest object, but nothing tells them what to frame or what the app will do with it, so the first capture is a guess. On a real photograph the emptiness is defensible; the missing part is the *framing guide*, which is the thing that makes a viewfinder a viewfinder.
- Fix: add a persistent, non-interactive framing guide to the preview — a corner-bracket frame at 1:1 / 4:3 in `hud` mint, plus a one-line caption under the "Location on" chip ("Point at one thing") that persists for the session instead of the transient "Hold for settings" text. The photo should stay the loudest thing; the guide is four thin marks.
- Suggested command: /impeccable shape

### [P2] Two navigation systems on the camera screen, with different glyphs and different names for the same destinations
- Location: `/` (camera panel) top bar vs the bottom pill — `src/screens/camera-screen.tsx:919-927` (`Clock`, aria-label "Places you looked at" → `onOpenHistory`) and `:826` (`aria-label="Your profile"` → `onOpenProfile`) vs `src/components/layout/page-dots.tsx:5-9` (nav labels "You" / "Camera" / "Places", the third using lucide `History`) and `src/components/layout/shell.tsx:109-113`.
- Evidence: `shots/a1/50-camera-rest-nohint.png` — a plain `Clock` glyph at top-right and a different clock-with-counter glyph in the bottom pill, ~200 px apart, both meaning "the places you looked at"; a filled avatar circle at top-left and a `UserRound` person in the bottom pill, both meaning "You". `shots/evidence/places--mobile--light.png` — the panel that the bottom-pill clock opens is titled **"History"**, while the pill calls it **"Places"** and the camera HUD calls it **"Places you looked at"**.
- Impact: H6 recognition, not recall: the user has to work out that two different clocks are the same door, and three different names refer to one destination. The camera screen ends up with eight interactive islands of which two are redundant nav, so the shutter does not own the screen. Casey's thumb reaching for the top-right clock and landing in History instead of the camera is the likely accident.
- Fix: pick one. On the camera panel, let the top-left avatar and the bottom-pill person be the same affordance (keep one), and replace the camera's `Clock` with the same `History` glyph the pill uses so the two match; rename the panel header from "History" to "Places" (or rename the pill to "History") so one destination has one word everywhere.
- Suggested command: /impeccable layout

### [P2] The report hero stacks three floating labels before the first sentence, and inverts the reading order
- Location: `/report/demo-eiffel` — `src/screens/report-screen.tsx:258-298` (article `-mt-24` over the hero, `kind` chip, H1, meta row) and `src/components/report/photo-hero.tsx:178-190` ("Tap the dots on your photo" hint pill).
- Evidence: `shots/a1/70-report-top.png` (390) and `shots/a1/81-report-narrow.png` (320): the first thing the eye meets after the photo is a 15%-green pill reading "Wrought-iron lattice tower" (the report's *category*), then "Eiffel Tower" (the place name), then "Paris, France · 1887–1889 · High confidence". The category chip therefore outranks the place name in the first read. On the hero's bottom fade band the chip is low-contrast green-on-photo. At 320x568 the hero takes 420 px (`min-h-[420px]`), the hint pill and the kind chip stack 17 px apart, and the meta row breaks leaving a dangling "·" at the end of line 1 with "High confidence" orphaned on line 2.
- Impact: "answer first, detail second" is inverted — the traveller reads the category before the name of the thing they are standing in front of. Two overlapping translucent pills plus a chip inside a 320-wide hero make the top of the report the busiest region of the screen, when the photo should be the only thing competing there.
- Fix: drop the "Tap the dots on your photo" hint to a single unobtrusive line, or auto-hide it once the user has opened one marker; move the `kind` chip below the H1 and the meta row (or fold it into the meta row) so the place name is the first text; at ≤360 px render the meta row as one wrapped line without the separate `·` spans.
- Suggested command: /impeccable layout

### [P2] Onboarding slide 1 has no visual anchor — a 15%-opacity logo floating in a half-screen void
- Location: `/` onboarding slide 1 — `src/screens/onboarding-screen.tsx:16-40` (`ViewfinderArt`: four brand arcs + `<Logo className="text-foreground/15">`), `:194-196` (art in a `flex-1` centred region).
- Evidence: `shots/a1/02-onb-1-light.png` — the art region occupies roughly 55 % of the screen height and carries a ~64 px green bracket and a pale grey ring whose only opaque part is the tiny green core; the ring is the Nekibuki mark rendered at `text-foreground/15`, so the brand's own signature is ghosted out. `shots/a1/03-onb-2-light.png` and `shots/a1/04-onb-3-light.png` — slides 2 and 3 carry a filled photo card and two ink-filled chat bubbles, i.e. the two slides with real mass. `shots/a1/01-login-light.png` — the same `Logo` component is a solid ink ring on the login, so the mark has two different weights across the funnel.
- Impact: the first onboarding slide, which is the one that has to make "point at anything" feel real, is the emptiest and least legible of the three; the ghosted mark looks like a rendering fault rather than a deliberate treatment. Because the three art pieces have three different sizes and weights (208 px bracket, 208 px card, 240 px chat), the set does not read as one system.
- Fix: render the logo at full strength inside the bracket (or use the bracket alone and drop the ghost ring), and scale all three pieces to one optical width so the slides feel like a set. If slide 1 needs more presence, put the viewfinder bracket around a real photo fragment rather than around an abstract mark.
- Suggested command: /impeccable onboard

### [P3] Trivia carousel is swipe-only with a clipped-word peek; profile uses destructive red for a non-destructive action and truncates labels
- Location: `src/components/report/trivia-carousel.tsx:60-102` (no dots, no arrows; `-mx-6 … w-[78vw] max-w-[320px]` cards, edge fade only) and `src/screens/profile-screen.tsx` ("Clear my places" and "Sign out" rows; `src/screens/history-screen.tsx:71-84` icon-only saved filter).
- Evidence: `shots/a1/11-report-trivia.png` — the next card peeks in showing the clipped fragments "DES" and "Gu", which reads as a broken render rather than an affordance; "1 of 4" is the only progress cue. `shots/a1/62-profile-bottom.png` — "Clear my places" (destructive, chevron implying navigation) and "Sign out" (not destructive) are both painted in destructive red, so red no longer means "destroy"; the row subtitle is cut mid-word at "Removes every saved guide and conversat…". `shots/a1/83-places-narrow.png` — at 320 px the profile rows truncate the *labels* rather than the control: "Language of …", "Welcome sli…", "Show the intro …".
- Impact: the carousel's affordance is below the threshold of visible and the peek actively reads as a bug; in Profile the destructive colour loses its meaning, and at 320 px the row that tells you what the setting does is the part that gets cut.
- Fix: show the peek as a clean sliver (increase the fade width or inset the next card) and add four small dots or a "Next ›" button under the carousel; colour only "Clear my places" red and leave "Sign out" in ink; at ≤360 px let the settings row wrap the control onto a second line instead of truncating the label, and shorten the "Clear my places" subtitle to a single short line.
- Suggested command: /impeccable polish

Minor observations:
- The saved-only filter in Places is an icon-only bookmark (fills solid when active); it reads as a "save" action rather than a filter, and it is the only toggle on the screen.
- Deep-linking `/report/demo-eiffel` on a cold profile shows the "This guide is gone" dead end (`shots/a1/81-report-narrow.png`, first capture) — the demo analyses only exist after the app has run and seeded IndexedDB once.
- The seeded demo data marks Blue Mosque and Eiffel Tower as saved, so the saved-only filter always returns two rows and the "No saved places yet" empty state is unreachable without un-saving both by hand.
- The floating chat composer permanently sits over the report's reading column; the last line of the intro paragraph is behind it in `shots/a1/70-report-top.png` and the first quick-fact tile is behind it in the 1280 capture.
- At 1280x800 the camera HUD's corner controls sit at the extreme left/right of a 1280 px bar while the shutter row is clamped to `max-w-sm` in the middle, so the top bar stretches to ~1250 px around a 100 px chip (`shots/evidence/camera--desktop--light.png`).
- On the report, the photo hero is full-bleed even at 1280, so the 42 rem reading column overlaps the hazy bottom of a 2.7:1 letterbox photo and the H1 appears to sit on the photograph (`shots/a1/81-report-desktop.png`).

Persona red flags:
- **Jordan (Confused First-Timer)** — lands on the camera with no hint left and cannot find torch/grid/zoom (only the sr-only button and a 420 ms hold exist); taps a photo marker expecting the photograph to be shown and instead gets the same sentence as the list below; and in Places cannot tell the icon-only bookmark means "show only saved" rather than "save this". The onboarding slide 1 art, being a ghosted logo, never demonstrates the "point at anything" idea it claims.
- **Casey (Distracted Mobile User)** — the whole app is one horizontal swipe away from three panels, but the swipe affordance is a one-time pill; the report's fixed chat composer covers the line being read at the moment it is read; and holding the middle of the viewfinder for 420 ms on a phone is a gesture nobody expects while trying to take a picture.
- **Riley (Deliberate Stress Tester)** — pasting `/report/demo-eiffel` into a fresh browser gives "This guide is gone / It may have been deleted from this device." with only "Back to the camera" (`shots/a1/81-report-narrow.png`); and the saved-only filter's promised empty state cannot be reached with the shipped demo data.

Questions:
- If the photo is the source of the guide, what should tapping a dot show that scrolling cannot — and if the answer is "the same sentence", why is the sheet a modal?
- Which of the camera's seven visible controls would survive if the product decided that the only job of the landing screen is "point at one thing"?
- The app has three names for one destination ("Places", "History", "Places you looked at"). Which one is the product's word, and why is the other two still shipping?

SUMMARY: 2/4; 0 P0, 2 P1, 4 P2, 1 P3; the camera's viewfinder settings exist only behind a 420 ms long-press and a 5-second one-time hint, so a whole class of controls is invisible to every touch user.
